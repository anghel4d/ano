# Ano — limitations, classified

What the demo suites surfaced (10-conways, 11-noita, the wand stress test), sorted by what kind of thing each finding is: a transpiler bug against a law the spec already states, a semantic gap where the spec rules and anoc does not yet follow, or an open design question the algebra itself still owes an answer. The first kind gets fixed without ceremony; the second kind is implementation debt with the spec as the contract; the third kind lives in ano-language.md's Open Questions and is only witnessed here, never resolved. Nothing in any category required changing the grammar.

## 1. Fixed — emitter conformance bugs, not language limitations

These were bugs in emit.c against laws ano-language.md already states. They are recorded so the classification is explicit; none of them was a parser error, none was a grammar quirk, none touches the algebra.

- Same-column `;` batch committed last-wins. §10 says batched effects observe the pre-state and must commute under the registered merge laws; the emitter computed every delta against pre-state and committed only the last, silently dropping a commuting write (two Damage Plus gave +10, not +20 — n2-modifiers.bqn pins 13 ≠ 23 as the counterexample). Now the additive family (`+=` `-=`) and the multiplicative family (`*=` `/=`) compose by rebasing the later accumulate onto the earlier commit — sequential composition equals the merge exactly when the family commutes — presence writes compose idempotently, and pair-field SETs compose when the fields differ. A pair with no merge law (`+=` beside `*=`, a double SET) is rejected at emit time: "no merge law: written twice in one barrier (§10)". The rejection is the compile-time conflict check §11 promises for the rule set, landed first at statement scope.
- One spawn slot per statement. A second `spawn` in a `;` batch silently replaced the first, so the one-barrier Double Spell minted only the bomb. Now each spawn effect appends its own row group in effect order and keys mint once across the batch; n1-cast.bqn's mint witness (one mint of two ≡ two mints of one) is the commutation that makes the batch deterministic.
- Scan-along write-back did not conjugate. `Spell , Bal = scan(+) X along rot` computed the scan in along order and scattered in world order — values landed in the wrong cells, an order leak into a Tier-2 write, precisely what the equivariance law forbids. The scatter now goes through the order's grade: sort, act, unsort — h(c) = f(c∘σ)∘σ⁻¹, the conjugation Tier 2 already licenses. Composition rule: a scan-along value keeps its order through scalar arithmetic (`1 + (scan(+) …)`); composed against any other column the emit fails, since the two orders admit no alignment.
- `scan(min)` silently emitted the max-scan (the step map fell through to ⌈`). Mapped now; an unregistered scan step is rejected instead of guessed.
- Entity row structure versus lattice relations. In a mixed world (entity columns beside a lattice with a stencil srel), a despawn tried to keep-filter the stencil's fibers with an entity-length mask and crashed. Row-structure commits now skip frame-foreign relations — a relation whose fiber count is not the entity world's n belongs to the other habitat and is never filtered or padded.

## 2. Semantic gaps — the spec rules, anoc does not yet follow

- The shared rule barrier (§11). All standing rules active in a tick gather one pre-state and the set scatters once. anoc runs each installed rule as its own barrier, and Life is the smallest program where the difference is observable: bloom/wither run sequentially break the blinker in both orders, differently (demos/10-conways/c3 pins the divergence; the two-rule flora pair, and Noita's spread-plus-consume and steer-plus-move pairs, all wait on this). The implementation shape is known: gather every rule's mask against the tick snapshot, stage every effect buffer, commit once — the `;` law lifted to the rule set, reusing the merge machinery of §10 that now exists in the emitter.
- Static admissibility of overlapping rule writes. §11 accepts overlapping writes only on disjoint footprints or a merge law. bloom and wither both write planted, and their disjointness certificate is the complementary guard literals (`!Planted` / `Planted` over one pre-state) — invisible to a column-level footprint check. Whether the installer reads guard literals as certificates, or the pair is rejected and Life must be spelled as the fused value write, is undecided. This one is half design: the conflict rule's vocabulary (footprints, merge laws) may need a third clause (guard complements), and that belongs beside §11.

## 3. Open design — the algebra owes an answer, witnessed but not resolved here

- The frame join. Part V's two habitats meet in one statement when a lattice predicate needs a parameter read from an entity column: the Noita detonation is σ over cells around the payload's pos. The resolved coordinate-frames entry already carries the mechanism — pos = φ(k) = o + S·k, with `@` fixing (o, S) per scope and the こそあど deixis carrying the origin — so the algebra has no obstruction: the entity's position is one mirror-read, a constant by the time the lattice gather runs, the same π-feeds-σ shape as `Faction = Player.Faction` moved from the value slot to the frame slot. What is missing is the surface: whether the frame anchor is a deictic alias re-resolved per evaluation (`@impact`, the cursor's-raycast pattern), a registered binding (`at rally`), or an inline mirror-read in the scope position (`Oil @ blast(5) at Firebolt.pos`) folds into the Explicit-bindings and dot/@ open questions. Interim convention, used by n5/n6: the material chunk names its own coordinates and the header says so.
- Identity across the barrier. The trigger rule's payload records parent = the carrier's gather-time row, and the same barrier despawns the carrier — extensional data outliving the intensional surface, exactly the spec's Identity entry. n4-trigger pins it as data; nothing new to decide here, but the wand made it concrete.
- Cross-block wand state. Within one cast block the evaluator is pure calculus — the draw is a scan (w1), wrap is modular index arithmetic on a regenerable ground (w2), modifier binding is a reverse min-scan (w3) — but the cast pointer BETWEEN blocks is a carried recurrence. The Recurrences entry already rules on this: the game loop is the scan, one barrier per tick, so a Cursor column advanced by a standing rule is expressible today, and the within-statement carry stays rejected. The w-series takes the pointer as data (w2's rot binding, host-supplied) pending the naru-advanced cursor.
- Correlated order joins on the surface. "Next projectile at or after each modifier" is per-row correlated: each row's answer folds a different suffix of an ordered line. The calculus holds it (reverse min-scan, w3's .bqn), and after the conjugation fix the emitter can even write one back — but the surface has no spelling that composes a scan-along into predicate position or takes an `order by` view as the `along` operand; the runnable twin used the collapsed case (a scoped fold when all modifiers precede all projectiles). Whether γ should extend to order-fibers (the fiber at slot i = slots after i, a geometry srel) or scan-along should be admitted in selection position is a surface-design question adjacent to the Recurrences and γ entries.
- Staging and quotation, still dormant. The Greek letters needed none of it: the wand is a table, so Alpha is a grade-and-take, Omega a replicate, and parent pins the reflection (w4) — reflection is free when the program is data. The edge stays where the spec's Staging entry put it: a spell that mints a spell-that-computes, rules writing rules, the koto nominalizer. The stress test never touched it.

The negative result worth stating plainly: the wand stress test — multicast grouping, blue-modifier chaining, wrap-around, Greek-letter reflection, Add Mana, Chainsaw — introduced no new operator, no new statement form, and no grammar change. Every mechanism landed in forms the spec already had: scan, grade, replicate, fold, γ, modular index arithmetic, the barrier, and the registry. The gaps it did reveal are the five conformance bugs (fixed) and the surface questions above.
